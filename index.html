Toyota Thesis Case Study

Automotive Paint Booth Environmental Control

MSc Thesis Grade: 19/20

Problem Statement

Atmospheric temperature and moisture fluctuations in automotive spray booths induce paint cure defects and process drift, violating Toyota's Ryohin Joken standards.

Architectural Solution

  • Built a Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) Quasi-Digital Twin linking physical Siemens S7-1200 PLCs with MATLAB Simscape thermal-fluid plant models via OPC UA.
  • Executed sensor placement optimization and FMEA protocols to minimize reading latency.

Quantified Results

  • Booth temperature stabilized to ±0.5°C and relative humidity to ±2%.
  • Pearson correlation coefficient between digital twin current draw and physical hardware exceeded r = 0.96.
Tooling Engineering

Injection Mold Design & Validation

Design Execution

Complete mold design developed in Siemens NX for an automotive ABS speaker housing featuring deep aesthetic undercuts.

Subsystems Built

  • Thermal Control: Conformal cooling lines engineered to balance temperature distribution and prevent warpage.
  • Calculations: Core/cavity stress analysis and ejector pin force calculations based on ABS shrinkage profiles.
CAE Simulation

Brass Drawing Process FEA Optimisation

Numerical Scope

Nonlinear finite element analysis utilizing Abaqus/Explicit coupled with Taguchi orthogonal arrays to optimize multi-pass deep drawing of brass alloys.

Technical Results

  • Mapped Von Mises stress profiles and die corner radius contact stresses.
  • Reduced post-forming material elastic springback by 18%.
  • Reduced peak punch draw force requirement by 12%.
